Teach-It Role-Play Cards

Ten kid cards and eight scenario cards for the Session S6 role-plays, plus paper ScratchJr blocks for rooms without tablets. The partner playing the child reads a kid card and stays in character; the coach may not touch the mouse.

10 kid cards 8 scenario cards Lessons 5.1–5.4 Session S6
How to run a round

1. The "child" draws a kid card, reads it silently, and holds the mouse (or tablet). 2. The coach may draw a scenario card, or just coach the project in front of them. 3. Four minutes. Coach with questions, process praise and "yet"; hands never on the mouse. 4. Swap roles and draw new cards. 5. Debrief: "Which line made you want to keep going?"

Play it real, not dramatic. The card describes a typical behavior; you don't need to act out a meltdown for the coach to practice.

Scenario Cards

S1
Scenario

"Can I share it publicly?"

Your child finishes a project and asks to share it with the whole Scratch community tonight.

Coaching goal: say yes to the excitement, then have the "before sharing" conversation: non-identifying username, no personal info, "show me any unkind comment."

Try: "Most comments are super nice! Here are our family rules first. Deal?"

Lesson 5.2
S2
Scenario

"I made this!" (they didn't)

Your child copied a game from the community, changed nothing, and calls it theirs.

Coaching goal: no shame. Remixing is good; crediting is what good Scratchers do. Add a thank-you in Notes and Credits, then make one change that's truly theirs.

Try: "Remixing is how lots of coders learn. Who made the original? What will you add to make it yours?"

Lesson 5.2 · digital citizenship
S3
Scenario

Sibling conflict

Two siblings are fighting over the one family computer. The younger one keeps grabbing the mouse.

Coaching goal: fair turns with a timer; invite the older child to the top of the scaffold ladder ("they teach"); offer the younger one ScratchJr as their own app.

Try: "You're the expert. Can you teach your sister how to make the cat talk, with her hands on the mouse?"

Lessons 5.1 & 5.3
S4
Scenario

An unkind comment

A comment on your child's shared game says "this is trash." They're hurt and want to stop sharing.

Coaching goal: treat it as a team job: read it together, report it, delete it, talk about feelings; consider turning comments off for a while. Feedback is information, not a verdict.

Try: "That comment says more about them than your game. Let's report it together."

Lesson 5.2
S5
Scenario

A selfie on the stage

Your child wants to use a photo of their face as a sprite and add their school's name to the title before sharing.

Coaching goal: keep the creativity, remove the personal info: a drawn self-portrait instead of a photo, no school name. Fine for a private project; not for a shared one.

Try: "Let's draw you as a cartoon hero instead. Our rule is no real faces or school names when we share."

Lesson 5.2 · personal info
S6
Scenario

All browsing, no making

Your child has spent the whole Scratch session playing other people's games in the community.

Coaching goal: nudge from "let's watch" to "let's make" without a lecture; agree session lengths up front.

Try: "Which one was your favorite? What would you change if you remixed it?"

Lesson 5.2 · healthy habits
S7
Scenario

Losing interest

After a tough week of bugs, your child says they don't want to do Scratch anymore.

Coaching goal: follow the fire. Suggest something silly or personal (their pet, a fart-sound piano), find an audience (a grandparent demo), keep it short and end on a win.

Try: "What's something funny we could make in ten minutes?"

Lessons 5.1 & 5.4
S8
Scenario

They've passed you

Your child asks how to make a high-score list, and you have no idea.

Coaching goal: model learning out loud. Find it together (Variables → Make a List, the Scratch Ideas page), and let them explain it back to you.

Try: "I don't know either. Let's find out together."

Lesson 5.4

Paper ScratchJr Blocks (no tablets)

Print in color and cut out. Pairs lay a row on the table left to right, then "run" it by acting it out: the coach reads, the "child" places the blocks. Build the lesson 5.3 row: Start on Tap → Hop → Grow → My Sound → Shrink → End. Write the number on each block in pencil.
